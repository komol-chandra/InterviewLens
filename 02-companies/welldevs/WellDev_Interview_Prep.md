# WellDev — Senior Software Engineer Interview Prep
Simple prep sheet. 50 items. Real info about WellDev's process is at the bottom (source: Glassdoor).

Format for each item:
- **Problem** = the topic in 1 line
- **Described** = short explain (3 lines)
- **Face** = what will actually happen to you
- **Test (3 steps)** = how to practice/check yourself
- **Solution** = what to do

---

## SECTION A — HR Round (8)

**1. Problem:** HR asks "Introduce yourself."
- Described: They want a short work story, not your life story.
  They check how you explain yourself.
  They check confidence and clarity.
- Face: You may go too long or too vague.
- Test: 1) Write your answer 2) Time it (60–90 sec) 3) Say it out loud, no reading.
- Solution: Use format: current role → key skill → why this job.

**2. Problem:** "Why do you want to leave your current job?"
- Described: HR checks if you badmouth past employer.
  They check if reason is growth, not conflict.
  They check honesty.
- Face: Easy to sound negative about old company.
- Test: 1) Write 2 reasons 2) Remove any blame words 3) Practice a neutral tone.
- Solution: Say you want growth/new challenge, stay positive.

**3. Problem:** "Why WellDev?"
- Described: They check if you researched the company.
  They check genuine interest.
  They check culture fit.
- Face: You may not know enough about WellDev.
- Test: 1) Read their careers page 2) Note 2 values (e.g. people-first, flexible work) 3) Link it to your goals.
- Solution: Mention their focus on mentorship/growth and Rails work.

**4. Problem:** "Tell me about a conflict with a teammate."
- Described: Behavioral question.
  Checks teamwork skill.
  Checks how you solve problems calmly.
- Face: You may only tell the problem, not the fix.
- Test: 1) Pick one real story 2) Use Situation-Action-Result 3) Time it under 2 min.
- Solution: Focus on what YOU did to fix it, end with good result.

**5. Problem:** "What are your salary expectations?"
- Described: HR checks your number vs budget.
  They check if you know the market.
  They check if you're flexible.
- Face: Saying too low or too high, or freezing up.
- Test: 1) Check market range (below) 2) Pick a range, not one number 3) Practice saying it calmly.
- Solution: Give a range, say it's negotiable based on full package.

**6. Problem:** "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
- Described: Checks long-term thinking.
  Checks if you'll stay.
  Checks ambition matches the senior role.
- Face: Sounding like you'll leave soon, or no plan.
- Test: 1) Write 2 goals 2) Link them to WellDev's growth path 3) Keep it short.
- Solution: Say you want to grow into tech lead/mentor role there.

**7. Problem:** HR situational questions ("What if a deadline is at risk?").
- Described: Checks decision-making.
  Checks communication under pressure.
  Checks ownership.
- Face: You may panic or blame others in the answer.
- Test: 1) List 2–3 past situations like this 2) Write how you handled it 3) Practice calm tone.
- Solution: Show you'd flag early, communicate, and re-plan — not hide the problem.

**8. Problem:** "Do you have questions for us?"
- Described: Last part of HR round.
  Checks your real interest.
  Bad to say "no questions."
- Face: Blank mind at the end of interview.
- Test: 1) Prepare 3 questions now 2) Pick from role, team, growth 3) Practice asking naturally.
- Solution: Ask about team size, tech stack, or growth path for seniors.

## SECTION B — Technical / Dev Team Round (12)

**9. Problem:** Live coding problem in front of dev team.
- Described: They give 1–2 coding problems.
  Checks logic and code quality.
  Checks how you talk while coding.
- Face: Freezing up, silent coding, messy code.
- Test: 1) Practice 5 easy-medium problems on paper 2) Explain out loud while solving 3) Time yourself (30–40 min each).
- Solution: Talk through your thinking, ask clarifying questions first.

**10. Problem:** Ruby on Rails specific questions.
- Described: WellDev builds with Ruby on Rails.
  They may ask MVC, ActiveRecord, routes.
  They check real project experience.
- Face: Rusty on Rails basics if you use other stacks.
- Test: 1) Review MVC pattern 2) Review ActiveRecord associations 3) Build 1 small Rails demo app.
- Solution: Refresh Rails core concepts, have 1 project story ready.

**11. Problem:** Database questions (DBMS).
- Described: Common WellDev MCQ/interview topic.
  Checks SQL, indexing, normalization.
  Basic but must be solid.
- Face: Forgetting normalization rules or SQL joins.
- Test: 1) Write 3 SQL join examples 2) Explain normalization in your words 3) Solve 5 SQL practice questions.
- Solution: Revise SQL joins, indexes, and normalization forms.

**12. Problem:** Operating System (OS) basics.
- Described: Part of the MCQ/CS fundamentals round.
  Checks process, memory, threads.
  Common trainee/junior fail point, senior should know well.
- Face: Confusing process vs thread, deadlock concepts.
- Test: 1) Review process vs thread 2) Review deadlock 3) Do 10 quiz questions online.
- Solution: Refresh OS basics 1–2 days before.

**13. Problem:** Design pattern questions.
- Described: Checks if you write maintainable code.
  Senior-level expectation.
  Common WellDev question area.
- Face: Only knowing pattern names, not real use.
- Test: 1) Learn Singleton, Factory, Observer 2) Find each in a real project you did 3) Explain each in 2 sentences.
- Solution: Know 3–4 patterns with a real example each.

**14. Problem:** Time complexity / Big-O questions.
- Described: Checks how you judge code speed.
  Common in MCQ round.
  Also asked in coding round.
- Face: Guessing complexity wrong under pressure.
- Test: 1) Review O(1) to O(n²) 2) Check complexity of your own past code 3) Practice on 5 problems.
- Solution: Say complexity out loud while solving, explain why.

**15. Problem:** System design question (senior level).
- Described: They may ask to design a simple system.
  Checks architecture thinking.
  Checks trade-off explanation.
- Face: Jumping to code instead of planning first.
- Test: 1) Practice 1 design (e.g. URL shortener) 2) Draw boxes before code 3) Explain trade-offs out loud.
- Solution: Always start with requirements, then high-level design, then details.

**16. Problem:** Git / version control questions.
- Described: Checks daily workflow skill.
  Common for team collaboration.
  Simple but must be fluent.
- Face: Forgetting merge/rebase differences.
- Test: 1) Review merge vs rebase 2) Practice resolving 1 fake conflict 3) Explain your normal git flow.
- Solution: Know branch, merge, rebase, pull request flow clearly.

**17. Problem:** API / SDLC questions (from real WellDev interviews).
- Described: MCQ often covers APIs and SDLC steps.
  Checks REST basics.
  Checks software lifecycle knowledge.
- Face: Mixing up SDLC phases or REST methods.
- Test: 1) List SDLC phases 2) List REST methods + use case 3) Quiz yourself on both.
- Solution: Memorize SDLC steps and REST verbs (GET/POST/PUT/DELETE).

**18. Problem:** Debugging a broken code sample.
- Described: They may show buggy code.
  Checks how you read others' code.
  Checks calm troubleshooting.
- Face: Rushing and missing the real bug.
- Test: 1) Practice reading unfamiliar code 2) Read line by line out loud 3) Time yourself finding bugs.
- Solution: Read fully first, then narrow down step by step.

**19. Problem:** Explaining a past project to dev team.
- Described: Very common in second round.
  Checks real hands-on depth.
  Checks how you explain technical work simply.
- Face: Too much jargon or too vague.
- Test: 1) Pick your best project 2) Explain it in 3 sentences 3) Practice for a non-technical listener too.
- Solution: Cover: problem, your role, tech used, result.

**20. Problem:** Two programming problems back-to-back (common WellDev format).
- Described: Reported by real candidates.
  Two problems, medium difficulty.
  Checks stamina and consistency.
- Face: Tiring out after problem 1.
- Test: 1) Practice 2 problems in one sitting 2) No break between them 3) Track your energy/focus.
- Solution: Pace yourself, keep same calm energy for both.

## SECTION C — Team Leader / Committee Round (8)

**21. Problem:** Leader asks about mentoring juniors.
- Described: Senior role expects mentoring.
  Checks people skills.
  Checks patience level.
- Face: No story ready about helping others.
- Test: 1) Recall 1 time you helped a junior 2) Write it in 3 lines 3) Practice saying it.
- Solution: Share a short story where you taught/helped someone.

**22. Problem:** Leader asks how you handle disagreement with manager.
- Described: Checks respect + honesty balance.
  Checks if you can push back professionally.
  Senior-level maturity check.
- Face: Sounding too submissive or too aggressive.
- Test: 1) Recall a real disagreement 2) Write how you raised it politely 3) Practice calm tone.
- Solution: Show you raise concerns with data, then respect final decision.

**23. Problem:** Leader asks about your weaknesses.
- Described: Classic trap question.
  Checks self-awareness.
  Checks growth mindset.
- Face: Saying a fake weakness like "I work too hard."
- Test: 1) Pick 1 real weakness 2) Add what you're doing to improve it 3) Practice saying it briefly.
- Solution: Give a real, small weakness + your improvement action.

**24. Problem:** Leader asks about ownership of a failed project.
- Described: Checks accountability.
  Checks learning from mistakes.
  Common senior-level question.
- Face: Blaming others or hiding the failure.
- Test: 1) Pick 1 real failure 2) Write what you learned 3) Practice owning it without over-apologizing.
- Solution: Own the mistake, explain the lesson, show it won't repeat.

**25. Problem:** Leader checks your communication style.
- Described: Senior devs talk to non-tech people too.
  Checks clarity, not just tech skill.
  May ask you to explain something simple.
- Face: Using too much jargon.
- Test: 1) Explain 1 tech topic to a non-tech friend 2) Time it under 2 min 3) Remove jargon words.
- Solution: Practice explaining tech ideas in plain words.

**26. Problem:** Panel/group interview (multiple people asking).
- Described: WellDev often uses group panel round.
  Checks how you handle pressure from many people.
  Checks eye contact / composure.
- Face: Getting confused on who to answer.
- Test: 1) Practice mock interview with 2+ friends 2) Answer each person directly 3) Keep pace calm.
- Solution: Look at the person who asked, answer them, then glance at others.

**27. Problem:** Leader asks "Why should we hire you over others?"
- Described: Direct comparison question.
  Checks confidence without arrogance.
  Checks unique value.
- Face: Sounding boastful or too modest.
- Test: 1) List 3 real strengths 2) Link each to the job needs 3) Practice a humble, confident tone.
- Solution: Give 2–3 concrete strengths tied to this specific role.

**28. Problem:** Leader asks about handling tight deadlines with team.
- Described: Checks leadership under pressure.
  Checks prioritization skill.
  Common senior scenario question.
- Face: No clear method to explain.
- Test: 1) Recall 1 tight-deadline project 2) Write how you split tasks 3) Practice explaining briefly.
- Solution: Explain how you prioritized, delegated, and communicated status.

## SECTION D — Exam / Test Steps (7)

**29. Problem:** Step 1 — Online MCQ test.
- Described: Covers APIs, SDLC, design patterns, math, time complexity, pseudocode.
  Usually done first, online.
  Reported as the hardest part for beginners.
- Face: Running out of time or unfamiliar CS basics.
- Test: 1) Take 1 CS fundamentals quiz daily 2) Time yourself strictly 3) Review wrong answers same day.
- Solution: Revise DBMS, OS, design patterns, and time complexity before this.

**30. Problem:** Step 2 — Possible IQ/logic test.
- Described: Some WellDev interviews include an IQ test.
  Checks pattern/logic thinking, not coding.
  Time-pressured format.
- Face: Getting stuck on 1 hard puzzle and losing time.
- Test: 1) Practice 10 logic puzzles 2) Skip-and-return strategy 3) Track your speed per question.
- Solution: Skip hard ones first, come back if time allows.

**31. Problem:** Step 3 — Personality test.
- Described: Some rounds include a personality assessment.
  No right/wrong answers.
  Checks culture fit, not skill.
- Face: Overthinking answers or trying to "guess" the right one.
- Test: 1) Read about common formats (e.g. work style tests) 2) Answer honestly in a practice run 3) Avoid extreme answers both ways.
- Solution: Answer honestly and consistently, don't overthink it.

**32. Problem:** Step 4 — One-on-one technical interview.
- Described: Direct interview with an engineer.
  Deeper dive into your code and experience.
  Follows the MCQ round.
- Face: Being asked to justify choices you made quickly in the MCQ or coding test.
- Test: 1) Review your own MCQ/coding answers after 2) Prepare reasoning for tricky ones 3) Mock this with a friend.
- Solution: Be ready to explain your reasoning, not just the answer.

**33. Problem:** Step 5 — Phone interview.
- Described: Often used as a quick screening step.
  Shorter, more casual.
  Checks communication and basic fit.
- Face: Sound distracted or unprepared on call.
- Test: 1) Find a quiet space in advance 2) Have resume/notes nearby 3) Practice a phone-only mock call.
- Solution: Treat it as seriously as in-person, quiet space, notes ready.

**34. Problem:** Step 6 — Group panel interview.
- Described: HR + dev team + leader together.
  Matches what you described in your question.
  Checks how you handle a mixed audience.
- Face: Unsure who is testing what (HR vs tech vs leadership).
- Test: 1) Prepare separate short answers for HR-style, tech-style, leadership-style questions 2) Mock with 3 friends playing each role 3) Practice switching tone smoothly.
- Solution: Answer each person in their "lane" — technical to devs, culture to HR, ownership to leader.

**35. Problem:** Step 7 — Background check.
- Described: Final step after passing interviews.
  Verifies past employment/education.
  Standard, not something to "prepare" content for.
- Face: Delays if your references or documents aren't ready.
- Test: 1) Prepare reference contacts now 2) Gather ID/education documents 3) Confirm references will answer calls.
- Solution: Have documents and references ready before this stage starts.

## SECTION E — Dressing Sense (5)

**36. Problem:** What to wear for HR round.
- Described: WellDev is described as hybrid, people-first culture.
  Likely smart-casual to business-casual expected.
  First impression still matters.
- Face: Overdressing (full suit) or underdressing (t-shirt).
- Test: 1) Check WellDev's social media/photos for office style 2) Pick 1 outfit 3) Try it on and check fit day before.
- Solution: Collared shirt, neat trousers, clean shoes — no full suit needed.

**37. Problem:** What to wear for technical/panel round.
- Described: Same principle applies for later rounds.
  Consistency across rounds looks professional.
  Comfort matters for coding rounds.
- Face: Wearing something new and uncomfortable on the day.
- Test: 1) Wear the outfit once before the interview 2) Check you can move/type easily 3) Check it looks neat on video/photo if online.
- Solution: Reuse the same neat outfit, tested for comfort.

**38. Problem:** Grooming basics.
- Described: Small things add to first impression.
  Hair, nails, ironing matter.
  Easy to forget under stress.
- Face: Rushing and skipping grooming on interview day.
- Test: 1) Prepare clothes night before 2) Set reminder for grooming 3) Check mirror before leaving.
- Solution: Prepare everything the night before, not same morning.

**39. Problem:** Online interview background/setup.
- Described: If any round is video call.
  Checks professionalism even remotely.
  Lighting/background affects impression.
- Face: Messy background, bad lighting, low camera angle.
- Test: 1) Test camera and mic before 2) Check background is clean/simple 3) Do a practice call with a friend.
- Solution: Plain background, good lighting, camera at eye level.

**40. Problem:** Accessories / overall look.
- Described: Keep it minimal and neat.
  Avoid loud colors/patterns.
  Simple looks more senior/professional.
- Face: Distracting outfit choices.
- Test: 1) Pick neutral colors 2) Remove flashy accessories 3) Get a second opinion from a friend.
- Solution: Simple, neutral, clean — let your answers stand out, not your outfit.

## SECTION F — Exam Results & Salary (10)

**41. Problem:** When/how results are published.
- Described: You mentioned results come from the Netherlands team (likely HQ/regional HR).
  Timelines can vary by stage.
  Real data shows hiring can take time.
- Face: Long wait and uncertainty after interview.
- Test: 1) Ask HR directly "what is the expected timeline?" 2) Note the date they give you 3) Follow up politely if no reply by that date.
- Solution: Ask for a clear timeline at the end of your interview, follow up once if it passes.

**42. Problem:** Average hiring process length.
- Described: On average, WellDev's full hiring process takes around 52 days across all roles based on public interview reports.
  This varies a lot by role.
  Senior roles may differ from junior/trainee.
- Face: Assuming it's slow when it might just be normal for this company.
- Test: 1) Ask recruiter the expected number of stages 2) Ask expected weeks for your specific role 3) Mark your calendar to follow up.
- Solution: Set realistic expectations — weeks, not days, is normal here.

**43. Problem:** No update after a while.
- Described: Common candidate worry.
  Doesn't always mean rejection.
  Internal process can be slow.
- Face: Anxiety and wanting to give up too early.
- Test: 1) Wait until the timeline HR gave you passes 2) Send 1 polite follow-up email 3) Wait 1 more week before assuming no.
- Solution: One polite follow-up is fine; don't send multiple messages.

**44. Problem:** Senior Software Engineer salary range (general market, Cambodia-based reports).
- Described: Public salary data (levels.fyi) shows senior software engineer total comp in Cambodia broadly around KHR 102M–192M/year.
  Ranges vary hugely by company size and role.
  Company-specific WellDev data is limited publicly.
- Face: Not knowing what's "normal" to ask for.
- Test: 1) Check levels.fyi/Glassdoor for the role+location 2) Compare with your current salary 3) Decide your minimum acceptable number.
- Solution: Go in with a realistic range, not one fixed number.

**45. Problem:** Local market context (broader Cambodia dev market).
- Described: General reports show developer salaries in Cambodia often range roughly $500–$2,000/month depending on experience and company.
  Senior roles are usually at the higher end.
  International/remote-facing companies may pay more.
- Face: Anchoring on the wrong comparison (junior vs senior numbers).
- Test: 1) Confirm which currency/country WellDev will pay in 2) Compare senior-level, not junior-level, figures 3) Adjust for your experience years.
- Solution: Compare senior-level numbers only, adjusted to your experience.

**46. Problem:** Negotiating salary after an offer.
- Described: Most companies expect some negotiation.
  Comes after you pass all rounds.
  Should be professional, not aggressive.
- Face: Accepting first offer too fast, or asking too aggressively.
- Test: 1) Prepare your target number and your minimum number 2) Practice saying it calmly 3) Prepare 1 reason (skills/market rate) to support it.
- Solution: Say your range politely, back it with market data, stay flexible.

**47. Problem:** Benefits beyond base salary.
- Described: WellDev states they offer subsidized lunches, medical insurance, life insurance for permanent staff.
  Total package matters, not just base pay.
  Useful to ask about during salary talk.
- Face: Forgetting to factor in benefits when comparing offers.
- Test: 1) List all benefits mentioned to you 2) Estimate rough value of each 3) Compare total package, not just base salary.
- Solution: Ask HR to clarify full benefits package before deciding on salary number.

**48. Problem:** Confirming currency and payment terms.
- Described: Company may operate across regions (mentions of Netherlands, Dhaka, etc.).
  Currency of payment matters for real value.
  Should be clarified before accepting.
- Face: Confusion about what currency the number is in.
- Test: 1) Ask directly what currency salary will be paid in 2) Ask about payment frequency (monthly?) 3) Confirm in writing/email.
- Solution: Get salary currency and terms confirmed in writing before accepting.

**49. Problem:** Deciding if the offer is fair overall.
- Described: Combine salary + benefits + growth + hybrid flexibility.
  WellDev markets itself as people-first with mentorship and hybrid work.
  Fair decision needs full picture, not just number.
- Face: Judging only on salary number alone.
- Test: 1) List salary, benefits, growth, flexibility as 4 factors 2) Score each out of 5 for you personally 3) Add up before deciding.
- Solution: Weigh salary + benefits + growth + flexibility together, not salary alone.

**50. Problem:** Final prep checklist before interview day.
- Described: Bring together everything above.
  Reduces last-minute stress.
  Simple checklist beats scattered notes.
- Face: Forgetting a document, question, or outfit choice on the day.
- Test: 1) Prepare documents + outfit night before 2) Review your top 5 answers (intro, why company, weakness, project, salary) 3) Sleep well, arrive/log in 10 min early.
- Solution: Use a simple night-before checklist: documents, outfit, 5 key answers, early arrival.

## Quick Reference: Real WellDev Interview Facts (from Glassdoor, 149–157 reports)
- Common stages: Skills test (16%), one-on-one interview (14%), phone interview (14%), IQ test (13%), personality test (11%), presentation (9%), group panel (8%), background check (8%).
- Average hiring process: ~52 days overall (varies a lot by role).
- Senior Software Engineer interviews were rated among the *easier* roles by past candidates (vs. Front End Engineer, which was rated hardest).
- MCQ round commonly covers: APIs, SDLC, design patterns, basic math, time complexity, pseudocode.
- Panel format often includes HR + 1–2 engineers together, matching what you described.
