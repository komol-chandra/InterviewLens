# 02 — Interview Phases & Timeline

> Built from the official **"Recruitment Timeline (approximate)"** infographic, cross-referenced with 30 real Glassdoor reviews.
> Dates below are **WellDev's stated approximate windows** — treat them as guidance, not promises. Reviewers reported the full process taking **2 weeks to 7 months (~4 months typical)**.

## Phase-by-Phase Playbook

### Phase 1 — CV Screening (25–29 July)
**What happens:** They shortlist resumes against the requirements (0–1 yr exp, graduating by Aug 2026).
**Reported:** Interviewers later ask about **projects on your CV**, so every line must be defensible.
**Your prep:**
- Tighten CV to 1 page; lead with projects + tech used + your specific contribution.
- Be ready to explain **any** project in 3 sentences: problem → your role → result.
- Remove anything you can't discuss confidently.

---

### Phase 2 — On-site / Online MCQ Test (1 August) 🎯 *the main filter*
**Format (from reviews):** **60-minute** timer, **40–60 questions**, delivered via a link / auto-submit form, **proctored** (camera + clipboard, Qualligo). "Above average" difficulty for beginners; strong time pressure.

**Topics (official guideline + reviews):**
- Programming: OOP, Design Patterns, API, Database, Networking, HTML/CSS, Programming Fundamentals
- DSA: Data Structures, Algorithms, Complexity Analysis, Recursion
- CS Fundamentals: Database, Networking, Operating Systems, Security, API/HTTP, General Knowledge
- Logical Reasoning: Analytical challenges, Simple Mathematics

**Real reported MCQ-style questions:**
- Subnetting: *"192.168.2.100, subnet mask 255.255.255.192 — what is the network address?"*
- How an API works; REST/HTTP basics
- OOP characteristics / principles
- Time complexity of a given snippet
- SDLC phases
- Basic math + GK + aptitude

➡️ **Full study plan for this round: see `03-onsite-mcq-test-plan.md`.**

**Key rule:** they **rank by score** and call top scorers first. Don't just pass — **maximize score**. Skip-and-return on hard questions; never leave blanks.

---

### Phase 3 — Phone Screening (9 Aug – 8 Oct)
**What happens:** Short recruiter/HR call about background, projects, experience, and expectations. Sometimes merged with the HR round.
**Reported questions:**
- *"Tell us about yourself and your projects."*
- *"Tell me about your current job."*
- *"Have you participated in any interviews previously?"*
**⚠️ Critical:** One candidate scored top on the MCQ but was **rejected here for weak English communication.** This call is a **communication gate.**
**Your prep:**
- Rehearse a **60–90 sec English self-intro** out loud until it's smooth.
- Prepare a 3-sentence pitch for each CV project.
- Take the call in a **quiet room**, notes nearby, speak slowly and clearly.

---

### Phase 4 — HR & Technical Interview (9 Aug – 8 Oct) — *1st Technical*
**Format:** ~45 min on **Google Meet**, usually **2 senior engineers**; sometimes a separate short HR behavioural piece.
**What they test:** 2–3 **easy** LeetCode-style problems + conceptual **OOP** and **Database** questions + **CV project** deep-dive + a few behavioural.
**Reported questions/problems:**
- Coin change (greedy) · Fibonacci series · reverse a string using a stack · detect cycle in a linked list · find duplicates using a map/hash · recursive function to reverse a number
- "Write OOP code creating inheritance"; characteristics of OOP
- SQL: "find highest salary from a table," subqueries, basic queries
- Sliding window problem
- HR: "Introduce yourself," questions related to your projects
**Your prep:**
- Solve the exact problems above until automatic.
- **Think out loud**, ask clarifying questions first, state complexity after.
- Have OOP (encapsulation/inheritance/polymorphism/abstraction) + SQL joins/subqueries crisp.

---

### Phase 5 — 2nd Technical Interview (16 Aug – 15 Oct)
**Format:** Often **20-min HR + 40-min technical** on Google Meet. Harder than round 1.
**What they test:** **Medium** LeetCode, OOP-based design, SQL queries, DSA topics; for some, light **CS networking + system design + DB optimization**.
**Reported questions/problems:**
- Merge sort (implement it) · balanced bracket sequence · hour-difference calculation from clock time · BFS/DFS · sorting algorithms
- "Find number of currency notes summing to a given amount" (GfG)
- JWT authentication in a custom web app
- Internet data transfer & networking mechanism; DB structure & optimization; a light system-design question
**Your prep:**
- Push into **medium** problems (sorting, graphs BFS/DFS, two-pointer/sliding window, stacks).
- Be ready to **discuss trade-offs and alternative approaches**, not just get an answer.
- Refresh networking basics + how JWT auth works end-to-end.

---

### Phase 6 — Final Interview (24 Aug – 29 Oct) — *Onsite*
**Format:** In-person at the office, reported as an **all-day (~10am–5pm)** session in **3 parts**:
1. **~1-hour coding test** — 3 easy-to-medium LeetCode-style problems.
2. **~30-min code review** — engineers read, run, and question your code → they care about **clean, readable structure**.
3. **~30-min conversation with the COO** — behavioural + light technical + culture fit.
**Reported problems:** coin change, Fibonacci, hour-difference from clock time, merge sort, "3 easy problems in the final round."
**Your prep:**
- Write **clean, well-named, readable** code — they will review it live.
- Be able to **explain and defend** every choice; handle edge cases.
- For the COO: be warm, positive, show ownership and eagerness to learn. Have 2–3 questions ready.

---

### Phase 7 — Verdict
**What happens:** Final decision, often routed through the **overseas/HQ team** → can be slow. Rejection/acceptance emails have been reported as **delayed**.
**Your prep:**
- At the end of the onsite, **ask for the expected timeline** and note the date.
- Send **one** polite follow-up if that date passes — not multiple.
- Have references + documents ready in case of a background check.
- Note: rejected candidates were told they **can reapply after one year**.
