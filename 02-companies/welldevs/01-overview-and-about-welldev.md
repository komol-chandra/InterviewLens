## 2. About WellDev

- WellDev is a **software development / product engineering company** with European roots (Netherlands) and delivery offices in **Dhaka (Bangladesh)** and **Phnom Penh (Cambodia)**. Final verdicts for candidates are often communicated by the overseas/HQ team, which is why timelines can feel slow.

### What real candidates say about the vibe (from the 30 reviews)
- ✅ Positive: *"They were very nice to me," "co-operative and professional," "friendly and smooth process," "the receptionist was humble… they were hospitable."*

## 3. The Recruitment Process — Overview

WellDev runs a **multi-stage funnel**. The official infographic lists these stages (full dates in `02-interview-phases-and-timeline.md`):

```
CV Screening → On-site MCQ Test → Phone Screening → HR & Technical Interview
   → 2nd Technical Interview → Final Interview → Verdict
```

Real reviews describe it more simply as **4 effective rounds**:

1. **MCQ Test** (online, proctored, 60 min) — the big filter.
2. **1st Technical Interview** (Google Meet, ~45 min, 2 engineers) — easy LeetCode + OOP/DB concepts + your CV.
3. **2nd Technical + HR Interview** (Google Meet) — medium LeetCode, SQL, DSA + behavioural.
4. **Final Onsite** (at office, ~10am–5pm) — 1hr coding test + code-review session + COO conversation.

> They **call candidates one-by-one based on MCQ score** ("they call you based on best scorer"). So the MCQ is not just pass/fail — **rank matters**. Score high.

## 4. Technology & Tools Used in the Process

| Where | Tool / Tech |
|-------|-------------|
| MCQ test | Online form / given link with a **60-min auto-submit timer**, **proctored** (camera + clipboard monitoring; **Qualligo** proctoring tool named by multiple candidates) |
| Coding interviews | **Google Meet** (video) + **Google Docs** (shared coding), LeetCode / GeeksforGeeks-style problems |
| Final onsite | In-person coding + code review on their machines |

### Technologies & topics they actually test
These are the domains that appeared across the reviews and the official MCQ guideline:

- **Programming / OOP** — OOP principles & characteristics, inheritance, writing OOP code, design patterns
- **Databases** — SQL queries, joins, subqueries, "highest salary from a table," normalization, DB structure & optimization
- **Data Structures & Algorithms** — arrays, strings, stacks, linked lists, hash maps, trees; BFS/DFS, sorting (merge sort), sliding window, recursion, greedy
- **Complexity analysis** — Big-O of your solution + alternative approaches
- **CS fundamentals** — Operating Systems, Computer Networks (subnetting!), Security, SDLC
- **Web / API** — how APIs work, REST, HTTP, JWT authentication, HTML/CSS
- **Aptitude** — analytical reasoning + simple mathematics + general knowledge
- **System design (light)** — only lightly, and more for stronger candidates

> **Note on stack:** WellDev has historically used **Ruby on Rails** on real projects, but the **trainee assessment is language-agnostic** — reviewers solved problems in Python, JavaScript, and others. **Use the language you're strongest in.** Don't cram Rails for the test.

## 5. What "Good" Looks Like (how to pass)

| Stage | What they're really checking | Your job |
|-------|------------------------------|----------|
| MCQ | Breadth of CS fundamentals under time pressure | Score in the **top band** — speed + accuracy |
| 1st Technical | Can you solve easy problems + explain OOP/DB basics + talk about your CV honestly | Think out loud, ask clarifying questions |
| 2nd Technical + HR | Medium problem solving + SQL + maturity | Stay calm on harder Qs; be a normal, positive human in HR |
| Final onsite | Clean code, readability, culture fit | Write tidy code; be friendly with the COO |

**The three things that get people rejected:** (1) failing the timed MCQ as a beginner, (2) weak **English communication**, (3) freezing / silent coding. All three are fixable with practice.
